A bet can look profitable on average. But what happens to your wealth when you keep playing?

What is ergodicity?

In a repeated game, ergodicity connects two ways of averaging the same quantity: over successive rounds, and across possible outcomes.

For a quantity that is ergodic, these averages agree in the long run. They need not match over a short run, and individual outcomes can still differ.

A closer look at the definition

A stationary process has the same statistical behaviour over time, as independent fair coin tosses do. The expected value, or ensemble average, weights each possible outcome by its probability.

For a stationary ergodic process, the time average of a quantity with finite expected absolute value converges to its expected value with probability one. This is a limiting result, not exact equality after a fixed number of rounds.

Based on Davide Di Conno’s essay and Section 5 of Ole Peters’ paper. Sources and the formal theorem are listed in the bibliography.
