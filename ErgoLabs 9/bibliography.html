Published research · Growth factors and time

Ole Peters (2011)

The time resolution of the St Petersburg paradox. Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A, 369, 4913–4931.

Read the paper ↗

How this source is applied

§6, especially equations (6.6)–(6.10), motivates analysing products through average log increments. §7 distinguishes a dynamical derivation from a logarithmic-utility interpretation of a mathematically identical expression.

The paper's ticket-price lottery has wealth-dependent relative exposure. This website uses a fixed-multiplier game and derives its results separately. Continued access to equivalent opportunities is an important assumption, not a universal fact.

The Logic of Risk Taking. INCERTO, Medium, 25 August 2017.

How this source is applied

The exit-threshold extension distinguishes interrupted participation from unstopped compounding. Its ruin-centred discussion is not used as a formal definition of ergodicity or proof of broader claims about economics or preferences.

Author's supplied essay · Project motivation

Davide Di Conno

How can the ergodic theory be used to maximise one's wealth growth? Student research essay; undated.

A public reading link is not yet available.

How this source is applied

The coin-toss discussion on p. 4 provides the starting example; pp. 5–7 motivate the separate treatment of irreversibility and exposure.

The prototype refines distinctions between growth rates and arithmetic time averages, approaching zero and finite-time bankruptcy, and conditional results versus universal claims about insurance or strategy.

Model assumptions, reproducibility & limitations

Baseline: $100; independent multipliers 1.5 and 0.6 with probability ½; full exposure; no costs, income, consumption, credit, pooling or exit. Fractional cents are allowed.

At round 100, H follows Binomial(100, ½). Wealth is $100 × 1.5ᴴ × 0.6¹⁰⁰⁻ᴴ. Finishing below $100 means H ≤ 55, with exact probability approximately 86.44%.

Mulberry32 provides deterministic pseudorandom draws. Same seed reproduces this implementation, not NumPy's sequence. Endpoint calculations are checked against heads counts.

Simulation illustrates the model; it is not a proof or statistical test establishing ergodicity. Long-run log-growth maximisation is an objective, not a universal definition of rational choice. Preferences, finite horizons and constraints can change decisions. Expected-utility theory is not disproved by this example.
