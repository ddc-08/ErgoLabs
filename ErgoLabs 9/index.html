What is ergodicity?

Ergodicity connects two ways of averaging: following one history over time, and averaging across the model’s possible histories.

In a stationary ergodic model, the long-run average of a suitable measured quantity along one history matches its average across possibilities, for almost every history. The quantity and the model’s assumptions matter.

A closer look at the definition

Stationary means that the process’s statistical properties stay the same over time. The average across possibilities is often called the ensemble average or expectation.

For a stationary ergodic process and a quantity with finite expected absolute value, its time average converges to its expectation with probability one. A finite history can still differ from that expectation.

This introduction draws on Davide Di Conno’s research essay and the time-versus-ensemble perspective in §5 of Ole Peters’ paper. The formal statement is linked in the bibliography.
